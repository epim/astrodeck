Open observatory control

The night runs itself. You just watch.

AstroDeck slews, centres, focuses, guides, shoots, dithers, flips the meridian and parks at dawn, then writes you a report. It also shows you where tonight's cloud is, so you can pick a target that stays in the clear.

Why it exists

Your rig, your source code.

Every part of AstroDeck sits above one small device abstraction. The sequencer does not know what brand your mount is. It knows a mount can slew, report a pier side and refuse. That is why you can swap a camera vendor without touching autofocus, and why a new driver is a few async methods rather than a fork.

Vendor neutral

No brand owns your night

Native drivers for ZWO and Player One cameras, ZWO mounts, focusers and rotators, and the Wanderer filter wheel. Everything else through ASCOM Alpaca, or a Windows ASCOM driver via the bundled COM host.

Standalone

Complete on its own

Star detection, autofocus, plate solving, polar alignment, guiding and sequencing all ship in the box. A rig can run with no other astronomy software installed anywhere.

Or alongside

Keep what already works

Point it at NINA through the Advanced API, or at an ASIAIR over its own protocol. That setup keeps driving the hardware while AstroDeck adds the touch UI, Flows, sessions and remote access.

Offline first

Works with no signal

The Sky Atlas ships a ~250 MB offline survey pack and falls back to it automatically. A dark site with no signal is the normal case.

Remote

One outbound connection

The scope dials a forward-only relay. No port forwarding, no NAT surgery, and every tunnelled request is re-authenticated at home rather than trusted because it arrived.

Apache-2.0

Yours to change

Read it, change it, run it on hardware nobody anticipated. Third-party backends can ship as separate packages and register themselves without a fork.

One clear night

What you get to sleep through.

One night, in the order it happens. You are asleep for all of it.

The part nobody enjoys

The flip you do not have to watch

Mounts lie about flipping. AstroDeck re-reads the pier side after the slew, and when nothing moved it keeps the flip armed, drops its own lead time and tries again at the meridian. You wake up to a night that is the right way up.

Across nights

Never re-count what you already shot

Set a target once and keep adding to it over weeks. AstroDeck scores every frame for HFR, star count and guide RMS, lets you overrule any call it makes, and re-arms itself at dusk when the target rises again. Come back in a month to one finished stack.

The surface

Eleven screens, one rig.

Capture

Live preview with zoom, pan and a 1:1 loupe. Interactive histogram and stretch, star overlay, clip mask, filmstrip. Cooler with an at-target badge and dew heater.

Focus

V-curve autofocus on real HFR, hyperbola fit with a visible R². Per-filter offsets, refocus on temperature or frame count, and a Bahtinov aid with a live spike overlay.

Mount

Touch slew pad with a dead-man's switch, catalog goto with a below-horizon guard, plate solve and sync, tracking rates, park and unpark.

Guide

Built-in engine, PHD2 or NINA's. RA/Dec error graph, scatter, RMS and dithering. The Guiding Assistant measures seeing and Dec backlash and suggests settings.

Atlas

Frame the shot before you drive anywhere. Drag and rotate your real field of view over survey imagery, plan a mosaic, and do all of it at a dark site with no signal.

Tonight

Cloudy for a week and out of ideas? Tonight ranks what is up right now and tags it by difficulty, with altitude curves, transit, twilight and moon separation.

Monitor

ETA, progress, cooler, guide RMS, meridian countdown, HFR trend, live thumbnail and the sky dome. One tap turns the whole interface dark-adaptation red.

Align

Three-point polar alignment with a live error readout, plus a measured guide-scope offset so the guider knows where it is pointed.

Gallery and reports

Full FITS headers, per-frame WCS written back after a solve, an end-of-night report, and a stacking bundle pre-sorted for PixInsight, Siril or APP.

Try it now

Try it in two minutes, with no hardware.

Open Equipment, press Simulator rig, and eleven devices connect in about three seconds.

From source 
cd server
python -m venv .venv
.venv\Scripts\pip install -e .
.venv\Scripts\python -m astrodeck

Then open http://localhost:8800.

Or Docker, Raspberry Pi included 
docker compose up -d

Multi-arch, so the same setup runs on a mini-PC or a Pi 4/5. There is also a single binary for Windows, Linux and Mac with no Python and no Node.

Be fluent before your next clear night

The simulator renders a real star field that answers to where the mount points, where the focuser sits and which filter is in the light path. Autofocus walks a real V curve, the solver solves and the guider guides, so a two-target night runs to completion while it rains outside.

Put the blackout filter in and the frame goes dark, because the simulator knows what a blackout filter does. What you learn indoors carries over to the first clear night.

v0.3, on a real rig most clear nights

What is not finished.

  • Guiding and plate solving work and still have sharp corners. Used every session; both can still fail with a message that could be clearer.
  • No flats wizard. You can shoot flats, with auto-exposure and a calibration library behind them, but nobody has built the guided walkthrough.
  • The ASIAIR backend has never touched real hardware. Written against the protocol, tested against a fake.
  • The cloud model needs a GOES footprint. The Americas are covered; elsewhere AstroDeck says so rather than drawing an empty sky as clear.
  • Binaries are not code-signed, so Windows and macOS both warn on first run.
