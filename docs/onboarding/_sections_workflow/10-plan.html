10 Planning

Plan the night, then let it run

Choose targets that are high and dark during your imaging window, set exposures per filter, and queue a sequence that runs unattended.

Altitude
Image a target near its highest point (the meridian), where its light cuts through the least air.
Meridian flip
A German equatorial mount must swap sides as the target crosses the meridian (when the hour angle HA = LST − RA reaches 0).
Budget
Total integration = sub length × frame count, split across filters.
Safety
The sequence handles refocus, dither, the flip, and aborts on its own.

Best signal near the meridian; the run flips the mount as the target crosses it.

Pick a target too low (or behind a tree), or run past the meridian without a flip, and you lose the subs — or crash the mount into the pier.

A preflight altitude check warns on targets that never rise; the mount-altitude floor and pier guard abort a slew that would hit the pier; the flip is automatic and safety fails closed — a stale reading pauses the run.

SequenceView holds the Plan (frames, integration), a Targets list, and Automation (meridian flip, HFR spike rejection, dither, filter offsets).

Most imagers plan before dark; it sits last here because it's the part you keep tuning all night.
