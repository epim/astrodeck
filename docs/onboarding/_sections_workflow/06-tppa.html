06 Polar Alignment

Three-point polar alignment

Refine the mount-axis-to-pole error by plate-solving three points along a slow rotation in RA — no need to actually see the pole star.

Why
Tracking spins one axis; if that axis isn't on the true pole, the field slowly rotates and stars trail.
Three points
Solving the sky at three RA positions fixes the rotation axis relative to the true celestial pole.
No Polaris needed
It's computed from any solvable star field, not a sight line to Polaris.
Result
Azimuth and altitude error in arcminutes; you turn the mount's alt/az knobs to drive them toward zero.

Three solved points define the spin axis; the reticle shows how far it misses the pole.

Skip it and long exposures show field rotation — stars trail in little arcs even with guiding running perfectly.

AstroDeck drives a TPPA routine (via NINA) and publishes one polar reading — az / alt / total error in arcmin — to a live reticle, with pause/resume.

The old way needed a clear sightline to Polaris; plate-solving doesn't — good news for everyone south of the equator, behind a tree, or otherwise invited to check their privilege.
