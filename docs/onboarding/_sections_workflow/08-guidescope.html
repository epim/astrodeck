08 Guiding Optics

Why the guide scope isn't the main scope

Guiding gets its own small, short-focal-length scope and camera, separate from the imaging train.

Wide field
A short guide scope sees a wide patch of sky, so it almost always finds a usable guide star.
Main scope
Long focal length, narrow field, tuned for the target — a poor place to go hunting for guide stars.
Cost vs flexure
A piggybacked guide scope is cheap and light, but any flex between the two tubes shows up as drift.
Alternative
An off-axis guider (OAG) picks a star out of the main scope's own light, removing flexure, at the cost of a dim, cramped field.

Wide guide field, narrow imaging field — same sky, different jobs.

Differential flexure makes guiding look perfect on its own star while the main image quietly drifts.

Software can't remove flexure — dithering between subs and frame rejection hide slow drift in the stack, but a stiff mounting or an off-axis guider is the real fix.

AstroDeck resolves the guider role and its guide camera separately from the imaging camera.

The guide camera is the cheapest, ugliest sensor in the rig, and it never has to look pretty.
