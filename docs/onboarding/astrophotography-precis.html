02 Pointing the Mount

Mount + tracking

A motor spins one tilted axis at the sky's own rate, so a chosen star stays pinned while the Earth turns under it.

Drift
Earth rotates ~15°/hr; uncorrected, every star slides across the frame.
Fix
An equatorial mount tilts one axis parallel to Earth's; a motor turns it at sidereal rate to cancel the drift.
Coords
RA = sky longitude, Dec = sky latitude. A go-to maps a target to a mount move.
Reality
Tracking is close but imperfect — good for short subs, not flawless for long ones.
locked target tracking: off

Without tracking a multi-second exposure smears every star into a streak; the longer the sub, the worse the trail.

AstroDeck's mount panel: slew (move now), track (hold sidereal), go-to (point at a named target).

You are bolting a camera to a clock and asking it to undo the planet.

03 Aiming the Axis

Polar alignment

Pointing the mount's spin axis at the celestial pole (near Polaris, north) so tracking actually cancels the drift.

Target
The celestial pole — within a degree of Polaris in the north.
Goal
Dial alt/az to a bullseye until error < ~30 arcsec.
Method
A routine plate-solves the sky to measure the error; you turn two knobs to zero it.
misaligned → aligned
bullseye error

Left: an arc means error — the pinpoint is what aligned looks like. Right: nudge the dot into the center ring.

Error rotates the whole field around the pole; stars smear into arcs that no later processing can salvage.

AstroDeck's polar bullseye screen: an amber error-dot, rings that tighten as the error drops, and live alt/az nudge cues until you hit center.

You are aiming at a hinge you cannot see, by adjusting two screws you can barely reach.

04 Getting Sharp

Focusing

In focus = the tightest star dot, judged by a number (HFR / FWHM, smaller is sharper), never by eye.

Metric
HFR / FWHM — the star's width in pixels. Smaller = sharper.
Drift
As the night cools, metal contracts and focus wanders — refocus periodically.
Bahtinov
A mask throws 3 diffraction spikes; the center spike sits dead-center when focused.
V-curve
Autofocus steps the focuser, plots HFR vs position, and drives to the minimum.
blur → Bahtinov lock
V-curve autofocus

Soft focus is unrecoverable — bloated stars and lost faint detail bake into every frame and no later step sharpens them back.

AstroDeck's focus panel: live HFR readout, a V-curve run, and a Bahtinov auto-focus flow that reads the spikes for you.

The sharpest moment of your night is decided by a number you can't see and a wrench you keep forgetting.

05 Quieting the Sensor

Sensor cooling

A thermoelectric cooler holds the sensor at a fixed cold temperature, keeping dark-current noise low and calibration frames repeatable.

Noise source
Dark current — self-generated; roughly doubles every ~6°C, grows with exposure time.
Set-point
A stable target like -10°C; lower and steady beats merely cold.
Floor
TEC reaches roughly 30-40°C below ambient.
Ramp
Cool and warm gradually to avoid condensation and thermal shock.
COOLINGLOCKED -10°

Grain clears as the sensor falls to its set-point and the cooler locks.

Drift the temperature between subs and your library of dark frames no longer matches, so calibration leaves noise it was meant to subtract.

AstroDeck's cooler box shows target vs. actual with a cooling-state indicator that reads LOCKED once it holds.

It is a tiny fridge bolted to a camera, sulking at physics.

06 Composing the Shot

Framing + FOV

How much sky lands on the sensor, and where the target sits inside it.

FOV
Scales as sensor size / focal length — long FL gives a tight crop.
Pixel scale
206 × pixelµm / FL mm arcsec per pixel.
Sampling
Match pixel scale to seeing; off either way wastes the data.
Big targets
Tile multiple panels into a mosaic.

A longer focal length tightens the crop; a plate-solve snaps the frame onto the target.

600 mm

Guess the FOV and the target spills past the sensor edge, or sits lost in a sea of empty sky you can't recover in processing.

AstroDeck's sky-atlas overlays the live FOV box on the target and lays out mosaic panels with overlap.

It is a viewfinder for a camera pointed at things you can't see yet.

07 Rotating the Frame

Camera angle

Turning the camera in its holder sets the position angle — the rotation of the frame on the sky.

Sets
Composition — orientation of the target within the frame.
By hand
Twist the camera and lock the holder.
Motorized
A rotator drives PA to a commanded angle.
Truth
Plate-solving reports the actual PA, in degrees.

The frame rotates until the edge-on galaxy lies along the diagonal; PA settles on the plan.

Skip the angle and a mosaic's panels meet at mismatched rotations, so the seams never line up and the stitch fails.

AstroDeck shows a rotation control with planned vs. actual PA, nudging until the error closes.

It is a Dutch angle you have to justify to a plate solver.

08 Holding the Star

Guiding + dithering

A second camera watches one star and pulse-corrects the mount many times a minute to hold a long exposure steady.

Why drift
Imperfect gears plus atmosphere pull tracking off over minutes.
Rate
Corrections fire many times per minute from the guide frames.
Target
Hold error to < 1″ RMS for clean stars.
Dither
Shift a few pixels between subs so stacking rejects hot pixels and patterns.
GUIDE STAR
RMS ERROR 0.6"

The star jitters and gets nudged back to center; the error trace rides near zero, then a dither hop offsets the next sub.

Let the error climb past an arcsecond and round stars stretch into little commas across every sub in the stack.

AstroDeck's guiding graph plots live RA/Dec error and RMS, with one-click dither between subs.

A robot playing the world's slowest game of keep-the-dot-centered.

09 Knowing Where You Are

Plate solving

Match a frame's star pattern to a catalog to get exact RA/Dec, rotation, and image scale — GPS for the scope, built from the picture it just took.

Input
One quick exposure; the detected star geometry is hashed and looked up all-sky.
Output
Where it points, how the camera is rotated, and arcsec/px scale.
With mount
Go-to-and-center: shoot, solve, correct, repeat — centered in 2–3 passes.
Underpins
Polar-align routines, framing, and post-flip target recovery.

A solve fails on cloud, a smeared frame, or the wrong image scale — and a solver that gives up in silence reads as a hang, blocking the whole night.

AstroDeck shows live solve and go-to-and-center feedback, and names the failure plus the next thing to try.

It finds itself by looking at the photo it just took.

10 Stacking & Calibration

Exposure + calibration

One sub is mostly noise. Stack many and the random grain averages away while real detail accumulates.

Why stack
Noise drops as ~√N — 4× the subs halves it, 100× cuts it tenfold.
Integration
Sub length × count. 5min × 60 = 5h total.
Darks
Lens capped (no light), same exposure + temp — maps the sensor's thermal pattern (dark current, amp glow, hot pixels).
Flats
Even-lit blank field — divides out dust shadows + vignetting.
Bias / dark-flats
Very short, capped frames — capture the sensor's fixed bias offset / read pattern.

Skip calibration and the fixed errors survive stacking: amp glow, dust donuts, and vignetting bake straight into the final image.

AstroDeck's sequence settings set sub length and count, and the calibration plan schedules darks/flats or pulls a matched library.

It is averaging your way out of a bad photo, sixty times over.

11 Running the Night

Sequencing + safety

A sequence runs the whole night unattended — acquire, focus, dithered subs, filter swaps — then parks at dawn.

Meridian flip
On a German equatorial, when the target crosses the meridian (the overhead line) the tube must swing to the mount's other side, then re-solve, re-center, re-calibrate.
Dithering
Nudge a few pixels between subs so fixed sensor specks don't stack into streaks.
Safety monitor
Watches cloud, dawn, dome close — pauses or aborts on unsafe.
Hard rule
Never slew at the Sun. Park the mount at the end.
safe · imaging

Miss the flip and the scope drives into the tripod, or tracking reverses and every sub after it trails — the second half of the night is scrap.

AstroDeck's sequencer chains the night; the safety monitor pauses on unsafe and the run ends at park.
