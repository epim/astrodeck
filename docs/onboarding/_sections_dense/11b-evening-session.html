Worked Example

One evening, every dial turned in order

Each preceding section was one instrument on the dashboard. Here they run as a single night — November, a backyard, a 130mm refractor on a German equatorial mount, a cooled mono camera, NGC 891 (an edge-on galaxy in Perseus, mag 10, transiting near midnight). Real numbers, real order, real failure points. The clock below advances as you scroll.

The beats above run moment to moment; the ledger below is the same night at a glance. One texture the strip can't hold: at the meridian flip the scope swaps to the west side and guiding has to re-calibrate, because east and west feel like two different mounts to it.

  • 20:15Cool to −10 °C, ramped over 4 min — low dark current, no thermal shock.
  • 20:25Polar align to under a couple of arcminutes — tight enough that field rotation over one sub stays sub-pixel, the residual guiding can't fix.
  • 20:40Plate-solve + center on NGC 891; 8′ off → corrected to dead center.
  • 20:48V-curve autofocus → HFR 1.8; rotate to PA 67°, re-solve to confirm.
  • 21:00Guide calibrates then locks at 0.6″ RMS. Sequence starts: 300s, dither ×3.
  • 23:55Meridian flip at the south crossing — re-solve, re-center, re-calibrate guiding.
  • 02:30Refocus after a 4 °C drop drifts the focuser; HFR back to 1.8.
  • 05:00End → park → warm cooler. Result: 48 × 300s = 4h on NGC 891.

What AstroDeck owes this night: twelve surfaces that handed off cleanly without you re-deciding anything you already decided. The cooler page, the polar-align solver, plate-solve-and-center, the V-curve focuser, the rotator readout, the guiding graph, the sequencer, the safety monitor, the park button — each is one beat above, and the seams between them are where a night quietly dies. A flip that doesn't re-solve, a refocus that never fires, a park that forgets the Sun. The job of the UI is to make those handoffs legible enough that you can be asleep for most of them.
