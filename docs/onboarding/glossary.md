# AstroDeck Glossary

Plain-language definitions for the astrophotography and AstroDeck terms used across the onboarding docs. The personas flagged these as "used before defined" or "assumed knowledge" — look them up here rather than hunting across four docs.

> **The two units everything hinges on.** Errors and sizes are quoted in **arcminutes** and **arcseconds**. A full moon is about **30 arcminutes** (30′) across. One arcminute = 60 **arcseconds** (60″). So an arcsecond is 1/1800 of a moon-width — tiny. When guiding reports "0.6″ RMS" or polar error reads "0.4′", that's how small the numbers are that decide whether stars are round.

---

## The sky & coordinates
- **Celestial pole** — the point in the sky the Earth's rotation axis points at; everything appears to rotate around it. In the northern hemisphere it sits near Polaris.
- **RA / Right Ascension** — sky "longitude." Measured in hours (0–24h).
- **Dec / Declination** — sky "latitude." Measured in degrees (−90° to +90°); the pole is +90°.
- **Alt / Altitude** — height above the horizon, 0° (horizon) to 90° (overhead).
- **Az / Azimuth** — compass direction along the horizon (0° = north).
- **Sidereal rate** — the speed the sky appears to rotate (one turn per ~23h56m). A tracking mount turns at this rate to hold a star still.
- **Meridian** — the imaginary north–south line overhead; a target is highest (best) as it crosses it.
- **HA / Hour Angle** — how far a target is from the meridian, `HA = LST − RA` (LST = local sidereal time). `HA = 0` means the target is on the meridian.
- **Seeing** — atmospheric blur; sets the smallest star size you can resolve regardless of focus.

## Mount & pointing
- **Mount** — the motorized head that aims and tracks the telescope.
- **GEM / German Equatorial Mount** — the common mount type that must **flip sides** when a target crosses the meridian.
- **Polar alignment** — aiming the mount's spin axis at the celestial pole so one motor can track all night. (Step 6.)
- **TPPA / Three-Point Polar Alignment** — a routine that measures polar-alignment error by plate-solving three points along a rotation — no view of Polaris needed. Reports **azimuth** and **altitude** error in arcminutes.
- **GoTo** — telling the mount to slew to a named target using its internal pointing model.
- **Slew** — the mount moving (fast) to a new position.
- **Sync** — telling the mount "you are actually pointing *here*," correcting its model. Usually driven by a plate-solve.
- **Plate-solve** — identifying exactly where an image points by matching its star pattern to a catalog ("GPS for the telescope, from the picture it just took").
- **Park / Unpark** — sending the mount to a known safe rest position / releasing it.
- **Pier side** — which side of the mount the scope is on (east/west); flips at the meridian.
- **Meridian flip** — the mount swapping sides as a target crosses the meridian, to avoid the scope hitting the tripod/pier.
- **Position Angle (PA)** — the rotation of the camera/frame relative to the sky.

## Camera & cooling
- **Sub / sub-exposure** — one individual exposure frame. You stack many subs into a final image.
- **Integration** — total exposure time = sub length × number of subs.
- **TEC / thermoelectric cooler** — the camera's built-in cooler that lowers sensor temperature.
- **Set-point** — the target sensor temperature (e.g. −10 °C) the cooler holds.
- **Dark current** — thermal noise generated by a warm sensor; roughly halves per ~6 °C of cooling.
- **Mono camera** — a black-and-white sensor; you shoot color one filter at a time and combine.
- **OSC / One-Shot Color** — a color sensor (Bayer matrix) that captures color in one shot.
- **Gain / Offset / Binning** — sensor sensitivity / black-level pedestal / pixel-grouping (2×2 etc.).

## Focus
- **HFR / Half-Flux Radius** — a measure of star size; smaller = sharper focus.
- **FWHM / Full-Width Half-Maximum** — a related star-size measure.
- **V-curve** — a plot of HFR vs focuser position; autofocus finds its minimum.

## Guiding
- **Guiding** — a second camera watching one star and pulsing tiny mount corrections to cancel residual drift. (Step 7.)
- **PHD2** — the standard guiding engine AstroDeck drives.
- **RMS** — the residual guiding error in arcseconds; smaller means rounder stars.
- **Guide scope** — a small, short-focal-length scope dedicated to guiding.
- **OAG / Off-Axis Guider** — picks a guide star out of the main scope's own light, avoiding flexure. (Step 8.)
- **Differential flexure** — the guide scope and main scope sagging differently, so guiding looks perfect while the image drifts.
- **Dither** — shifting a few pixels between subs so stacking rejects hot pixels and walking noise.
- **Hot pixels / walking noise** — fixed bright sensor defects that, without dithering, smear into streaks across a stack.

## Optics & framing
- **OTA / Optical Tube Assembly** — the telescope tube itself.
- **Focal length** — the optical length (mm); longer = more magnification, narrower field.
- **FOV / Field of View** — how much sky the sensor sees.
- **Pixel scale** — arcseconds of sky per pixel (`206 × pixel_µm / focal_length_mm`).
- **Sampling** — matching pixel scale to seeing; over/under either wastes data.
- **Mosaic** — tiling several framed panels into one larger image.

## Filters & color
- **Filter wheel** — a motorized wheel rotating filters in front of the sensor.
- **LRGB** — broadband filters: **L**uminance + **R**ed/**G**reen/**B**lue → natural color.
- **Narrowband** — filters passing a single emission line; reject light pollution but pass little light (long subs).
- **Ha / OIII / SII** — hydrogen-alpha, oxygen-III, sulfur-II emission lines.
- **SHO / HOO** — false-color palettes mapping narrowband to RGB (SHO = the "Hubble palette": SII→R, Ha→G, OIII→B).

## Calibration & stacking
- **Stacking** — averaging many subs; noise drops as ~√N (4× the subs ≈ half the noise).
- **Calibration frames** — darks (remove thermal signal/amp glow), flats (remove dust shadows/vignetting), bias (remove read pedestal).

## AstroDeck concepts
- **Backend** — how AstroDeck talks to gear: **simulator**, **Alpaca**, or **NINA**.
- **Alpaca** — a standard network protocol for astronomy devices.
- **NINA** — a popular Windows imaging app AstroDeck can bridge to.
- **Profile** — a saved mapping of each device role to a backend; auto-connects on boot.
- **Role** — a device slot: camera, telescope (mount), focuser, filterwheel, guider, switch, safety.
- **Safety monitor** — a device/logic that reports safe/unsafe (clouds, etc.); AstroDeck fails **closed** (stale reading = unsafe) and can pause/park the run.
- **Sequence / plan** — the queued list of targets and exposures the engine runs unattended.
- **Capability / role (RBAC)** — permission model: **viewer** (watch only), **operator** (image + guide), **admin** (everything). See `docs/ui-rebuild/03-personas-roles-attention.md`.
