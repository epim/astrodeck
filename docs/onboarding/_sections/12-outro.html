Where You Come In

Every idea here is a screen you can now critique.

You came in knowing software and nothing about the sky. That was always the right way around. Each concept from this primer maps to a specific surface in AstroDeck, and you now know enough about each one to look at it and have an actual opinion.

  • Polar alignment

    screen · the bullseye

    Get the mount's axis pointed true, or everything downstream drifts. The target is dead-center or it isn't.

  • Focus & HFR

    screen · focus / HFR

    One number says how tight the stars are. Lower is sharper, and you chase the bottom of that curve.

  • Cooling

    screen · cooler

    A cold sensor makes less of its own noise. You set a target temperature and watch it hold.

  • Framing

    screen · framing

    Where the target sits in the rectangle, and which way is up. A composition decision wearing a coordinate.

  • Guiding

    screen · guiding graph

    A live wobble chart of how well the mount is tracking. Flat and quiet is the whole goal.

  • Sequencing & safety

    screen · sequencer

    The plan for the whole night, plus the guard that stops it. The highest-stakes corner of the app.

Strip away the jargon and the craft is short: you are fighting motion, noise, and focus, on a rig that runs alone in the dark while its owner is asleep. That last part is the whole reason this is a software problem and not a knob-twiddling one. Nobody is standing there to interpret a vague reading or rescue a stuck step. The interface is the only adult in the field at 2am.

Which means it lives or dies on the things you are unusually good at judging: whether a half-awake person can read the current state in a glance and trust it enough to go back to sleep, and whether the rig fails gracefully and safely when a cloud or a cable or a missed flip goes wrong, rather than failing in silence and costing its owner a sensor. None of that requires knowing a single thing about astrophysics. It requires knowing software and people, which is your whole job.

Your move.

Go open AstroDeck and poke it with fresh, software-native eyes. You'll catch the control sitting in the wrong place before anyone who knows astrophotography would, and you'll notice the moments where a status is quietly a word when it ought to be a color, or a failure stays politer than it should. We built the rig. The interface is yours to argue with.

— and yes, the worm is allowed an opinion too.
